# Lesson 3 Exercises — Order, Subgroups, Generators

> Work one at a time. Convention: numbers = positions; $\sigma(i)=j$ = destination; composition right-to-left ($ab$ = first $b$ then $a$).

---

## Warm-ups (done in-chat)

- **W1.** Possible element orders in $S_4$. Enumerate cycle shapes of 4 points, apply order = lcm of cycle lengths.
  - $4 \Rightarrow 4$; $3+1 \Rightarrow 3$; $2+2 \Rightarrow 2$; $2+1+1 \Rightarrow 2$; $1+1+1+1 \Rightarrow 1$. Set of orders $= \{1,2,3,4\}$.
  - Caution: this equals $\{1,\dots,4\}$ only by coincidence; $S_5$ already has order $6$ (shape $3+2$).

- **W2.** Subgroup test checks:
  - (a) $\{e,(1\,2)\}$ — subgroup? (yes, $\cong C_2$)
  - (b) $\{e,(1\,2\,3)\}$ — subgroup? (no; not closed: $(1\,2\,3)^2=(1\,3\,2)\notin$ set)

- **W3.** Generators in $S_3$, $a=(1\,2\,3)$, $b=(1\,2)$.
  - (a) $\langle a\rangle = \{e,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)\}$, size $3$.
  - (b) $\langle a,b\rangle = S_3$ (all 6 elements). Two seeds (a 3-cycle + a transposition) generate everything.

---

## Q1 — All subgroups of $S_3$ (the formal exercise)

$S_3 = \{e,\ (1\,2),\ (1\,3),\ (2\,3),\ (1\,2\,3),\ (1\,3\,2)\}$, $|S_3| = 6$.

Find **every** subgroup of $S_3$. For each, give:
- the element list,
- its size (order),
- whether it is cyclic (if so, a generator).

Scaffolding:
1. The two trivial subgroups (smallest, largest).
2. The cyclic subgroup $\langle g\rangle$ generated by each non-identity element (5 elements, some coincide).
3. Lagrange: a subgroup's size must divide $|S_3| = 6$, so only sizes $1, 2, 3, 6$ are possible — use this to confirm completeness.

**Solution (learner correct).** 6 subgroups:

| subgroup | elements | size | cyclic? generator |
|----------|----------|------|-------------------|
| $\{e\}$ | $e$ | 1 | trivial |
| $\langle(1\,2)\rangle$ | $e,(1\,2)$ | 2 | yes, $(1\,2)$ |
| $\langle(1\,3)\rangle$ | $e,(1\,3)$ | 2 | yes, $(1\,3)$ |
| $\langle(2\,3)\rangle$ | $e,(2\,3)$ | 2 | yes, $(2\,3)$ |
| $\langle(1\,2\,3)\rangle$ | $e,(1\,2\,3),(1\,3\,2)$ | 3 | yes, $(1\,2\,3)$ |
| $S_3$ | all 6 | 6 | no (not cyclic) |

$\langle(1\,2\,3)\rangle=\langle(1\,3\,2)\rangle$. Lagrange: sizes $\{1,2,2,2,3,6\}$ all divide 6 ✓.

## Closing (in-chat, learner correct)

$\langle(1\,2\,3)\rangle\cap\langle(1\,2)\rangle=\{e\}$, mutually non-containing. Mini-theorem: subgroups of **coprime** orders intersect trivially ($|H|\mid\gcd=1$).
